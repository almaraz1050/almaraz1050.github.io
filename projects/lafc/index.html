LAFC & BMO Stadium: Fan Experience Analysis

LAFC Interview Project

04/2026

Project Context: As part of the interview process for the Business & Data Strategy Analyst role at LAFC, I was given a dataset of 9,246 fan survey responses collected at BMO Stadium from 2021 to 2025 and asked to present a hypothetical business plan to the club's President covering the next five years (2026-2030). The task was to analyze what fans feel about their experience, identify the most important operational and revenue opportunities, and walk through my thinking as if I owned the execution of the plan end-to-end.

Business Question: What do five years of fan feedback tell us about where LAFC and BMO Stadium should focus to grow revenue and improve the fan experience from 2026 to 2030?

LAFC enters 2026 with a loyal, sold-out fanbase and the most favorable external environment in the club's history. Two once-in-a-generation events are converging in LAFC's backyard within a three-year window, and BMO Stadium sits at the geographic center of both.

FIFA World Cup 2026
Los Angeles is hosting 8 World Cup matches this summer, with SoFi Stadium sitting 1.2 miles from BMO.
The US Men's opening match against Paraguay kicks off on June 12. The average World Cup visitor to LA is projected to spend $2,350 per trip, with hotel prices rising up to 90% during the tournament window. Of the 180,000 incremental visitors to LA County, 60% will be first-time visitors to the United States, a global audience seeing LAFC's city for the first time.
LA28 Olympics 2028
The 2028 Olympics Opening Ceremony will be held at LA Memorial Coliseum, directly adjacent to BMO Stadium.
37 of 41 Olympic venues are inside LA County, and the Games run July 14-30. The Southern California Association of Governments (SCAG) projects a $13.6-17.6B regional economic impact, with pre-Games spending already accumulating from 2024 onward. Infrastructure investment in the Exposition Park corridor will improve transit and pedestrian access surrounding BMO directly.
Fan Spending Power: FRED Data
LAFC fan spend grew 114% even as recreation costs outpaced income by nearly 10 points.
Federal Reserve (FRED) data shows recreation costs rose 12.7 index points since 2021 while real disposable income per capita grew just 1.5 index points over the same period. By 2025, real disposable income had recovered above the 2021 baseline, and LA entertainment sector employment reached a record 159,800 workers, 6.4% above pre-COVID levels. The spending environment heading into 2026 is stronger than at any point during the period this dataset covers.
LA Demographics & MLS Growth
LAFC operates in the largest Hispanic/Latino market in the country, and it is still growing.
LA County is 48.6% Hispanic/Latino, with 4.7 million residents, and Hispanic/Latino fans make up approximately 30% of the MLS national fanbase. 2024 was a record MLS season with 11.9 million total attendees and a $250M/year Apple TV deal raising the league's national profile. LAFC, valued at over $1.2B, is participating in a league-wide growth moment that the World Cup will amplify further.
The Bottom Line
LAFC sells out BMO Stadium at ~99% capacity every home match. Volume growth is not an option.
Every future dollar of revenue must come from per-fan spend, premium upsell, and non-LAFC event revenue. The 2026-2028 window brings the highest-spending, most globally engaged audience the club has ever had access to. High Priority
Fix the Three Persistent Pain Points Before World Cup Traffic Arrives
Parking, entry, and F&B have topped the complaint list for five straight years. Comparable LA venues have cut this friction with shuttles, the Dodgers from Union Station, LA Galaxy from two Metro stations, Intuit Dome's free electric shuttle. A BMO shuttle from the Expo Park/USC Metro station two blocks away is a low-cost fix with a hard deadline: summer 2026.
High Priority
Build a Tiered Premium Loyalty Program
The share of fans spending $400+ per visit grew from 9% to 42% between 2021 and 2025. A spend-based loyalty tier with early entry, dedicated F&B, and member pricing would capture more per-visit revenue and reward the fans already driving most of it.
Medium Priority
Convert Low Spenders Through Accessible Incentives
17% of fans in 2025 still fall in the low tier (under $175), and General seating alone drives 39.3% of revenue. F&B bundles at entry and mobile-ordering upsell prompts can move the floor of that spend distribution up without needing every fan to become a premium buyer.
High Priority
Treat FIFA 2026 and LA28 as a Fan Acquisition Window
180,000 World Cup visitors are projected for LA, 60% of them first-time US visitors. Capturing contact info at matches and building a World Cup-to-season-ticket offer turns a one-time visit into a long-term fan relationship. LA28 extends this window three years further.
Medium Priority
Expand F&B Mobile Ordering Coverage
Food & Beverage is the most-mentioned complaint by volume (2,021 mentions, 19.7% negative), driven by wait times rather than quality. Expanding mobile ordering coverage and upsell prompts is a throughput fix, not a menu one.
Lower Priority
Redesign the Survey for Longitudinal Tracking
Event type had to be inferred, spend data was missing for entire event categories, and the loyalty field was largely unusable. A standardized survey with a unique fan identifier is what makes real fan-value tracking possible going forward.
Methodology
  • Data source and cleaning: 9,246 fan survey responses (Jul 2021-Mar 2026) from BMO Stadium, cleaned in Python/pandas; event type was inferred via keyword classification since it wasn't explicitly labeled in the data.
  • Sentiment and theme analysis: scored open-ended feedback with TextBlob polarity and classified each response into one of ten operational themes via keyword matching.
  • Dashboard and external research: published a three-page Tableau dashboard (Sentiment, Pain Points, Fan Value) and grounded recommendations in outside research on the World Cup, LA28, FRED spending data, and LA demographics.
Takeaways
  • The most important analytical decision wasn't technical, it was documenting what the data couldn't tell us, the ACFC spend gap, the null loyalty field, the inferred event type, rather than treating them as footnotes.
  • Five years of the same complaints at the top of the list isn't a data story, it's an operational one. Naming that plainly is what makes the finding useful to a decision-maker.
  • Connecting the internal data to outside context, FIFA 2026 and LA28, is what turned a survey analysis into an actual business plan.
